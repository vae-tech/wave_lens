Framework demo & reference

wavefw WaveDrom JSON → interactive waves

A dependency-free renderer for the WaveDrom format with two outputs from one model: the interactive canvas viewer of the PSI5 datasheet (docs/reference/template.html) and a static WaveDrom-style SVG for figures. Waves are written by hand or converted from a simulator dump (VCD or FST) by the tools in tools/. Everything on this page renders from file:// with one script and one stylesheet. Step by step, with pictures: the guides.

1. Overview

wavefw.js parses WaveJSON (the input of the WaveDrom editor) into one time-ordered transition list per signal. Two painters draw that model with the same routine, so a figure and a viewer of the same data look alike:

  • WaveView: a canvas viewer with a name column showing values at the cursor, the wheel scrolls and ctrl+wheel zooms at the pointer (as in Questa), drag to pan, click to set the cursor, keyboard control, markers, jump buttons and groups you can fold.
  • WaveSVG: an inline SVG that follows the page's light/dark tokens (var(--wave-1), var(--wave-bus) …) and stays sharp when printed.

Two small extensions keep stock WaveDrom compatibility. "time" maps cycles to picoseconds, so the axis shows real time. The per-signal "tv" list carries exact transitions from a dump. Stock WaveDrom ignores both keys.

2. Quick start

<link rel="stylesheet" href="wavefw.css">

4. Interactive viewer


The same parser feeds WaveView. Without the time extension the axis counts cycles, starting at head.tick. Markers and jump buttons come from the optional markers and jumps keys. Click a group header (▾) to fold it.








Sorting signals


The Sort button cycles off → A→Z → Z→A (key s on the canvas). The field next to it lists name prefixes that are skipped when names are compared, so with i_,o_ the signal i_valid sorts as valid; the skipped prefix is drawn dimmed. Names compare naturally (d2 before d10), groups move as a block and their members are sorted inside, and a {} spacer splits the list into runs that are sorted separately. To start sorted, use "config": {"sort": "asc", "sortIgnore": ["i_", "o_"]} in the JSON, data-sort="asc" data-sort-ignore="i_,o_" on the element, or the settings file. Static SVGs take the same keys.








Highlighting instances


Each name row has a small mark ○ in its left gutter (it shows on hover). Click it, or ctrl/⌘+click the name, to highlight that signal or group (ctrl+shift+click another name: every row from the last one to it): a group covers all its members, nested groups included, so one click picks a whole instance. The highlighted rows stay in front on their usual background, their mark filled; the other rows fade to the background (the dim setting, 0.3) but stay visible, and the headers of the groups that contain a highlighted row stay in front too. Several instances can be highlighted at once. Highlight: N ✕ in the bar or Esc on the canvas clears it. To start highlighted, set "config": {"highlight": ["tx", "*.irq"]} in the JSON, data-highlight="tx" on the element, or the setting in a config file. Entries are full paths (tb.dut.u_tx), names, or * patterns. Static SVGs take the same setting.








Cursor time and window


The cursor group in the bar takes a time: type a number in the unit chosen next to it (ps, ns, µs, ms, s), or a time with its own unit such as 85.12us, then Enter or Set; the cursor goes there and the view follows when needed. The second field is a span: Enter or Show (c on the waves zooms in on the cursor, as in Questa; i o zoom in / out, f shows everything, l the window before the last zoom, as in Questa; the wheel scrolls the rows, ctrl+wheel zooms at the pointer, a drag with the middle button zooms into that range) shows a window of that width centred on the cursor. Both fields follow the cursor and the view while you are not typing in them; diagrams counted in cycles take cycles.



Fuzzy find


The find field in the bar (key /) searches like fzf / telescope in nvim: the typed letters in that order, anywhere in a name (rqv finds req … valid-like names, txd finds txd and tx_data). A word with a . runs over the full path, and words separated by spaces must all match. Three toggles next to the field, as in grep or VS Code: Aa (alt+C) the case matters, Full (alt+W) a word is the whole name (a.b: the end of a full path), .* (alt+R) the query is a regular expression (JavaScript, as grep -E; \<ready\> as in vim) on the name or the full path. Matched letters are underlined; Enter / shift+Enter, the ‹ › buttons or n / N on the waves (as in vim) go to the next / previous match, Esc anywhere in the viewer clears the search, every highlighted row and the selected signal, and leaves the view where it is (ctrl+C on the waves ends a search too) (switch Open off to get the earlier folding back). Copy puts the full paths of the found rows on the clipboard. Names selected with the mouse copy as clean full paths too (one per line), and with no search running ctrl+C on the waves copies the selected signal. The options apply while you type: Highlight puts the found rows in front and dims the rest, Open folds every module except those that contain a match (the earlier folding comes back when the search is cleared), and the colour next to Paint recolours the found signals and the highlighted ones (ctrl+click or the mark; a highlighted module: its signals; with neither, the selected signal; kept in this browser; Unpaint undoes it). Try val or bus.d in the viewer below.



Edge search and bookmarks


Every button, field and list of a viewer has a tooltip: what it does in a few words and its keys. ? at the right of the title line (key ? on the waves) opens a box with all the keys and the mouse, grouped by what they do, with the plugins' keys (sva.js, bus.js).


Click a signal name, or a row on the waves, to select it (↑ ↓ move the selection). ◀ ▶ in the bar (Tab / shift+Tab on the waves, as in Questa) move the cursor to the previous or next edge of the selected signal; the list between them (e) picks ↕ any edge, ↑ rising, ↓ falling, = value (a field: 0x40, 'h40, 64, -3, 'b101 or an enum label; the next / previous time the signal becomes it) or ✕ X / Z (the next x or z, of any shown signal when none is selected). A bus has no direction, so there every value change counts. With no signal selected the search takes the nearest change of any shown signal. + Mark (m) puts a numbered bookmark on the selected signal at the cursor, snapped to an edge within 6 px. Below the waves each bookmark has a line: its time (type 250ns, 85.12us, or a number in the unit shown; cursor moves it to the cursor), its signal (or — axis), the edge found there, a colour (the accent colour unless changed) and a text field for describing the event. On the waves, drag a circle to move the bookmark: left/right changes the time (snapped to an edge of that row), up/down moves it to another signal row or onto the time axis. Shift+drag from one circle to another draws an arrow between them; + Arrow adds one between the last two bookmarks. Each arrow has its from → to, a shape (WaveDrom edge shapes: - straight, ~ curve, -~ ~- curves bending late or early, -| |- corners, -|- step), a head (end, both, start, none), a line (solid, dashed, dotted), a colour and a label; click an arrow on the waves to edit its label. Any number of arrows can leave or reach one bookmark. An arrow's label goes into a thin lane of its own, inserted between the signals under the upper end of the arrow (above the row when both ends are on it), so the text never covers a wave; several labels at one place stack in the lane. An arrow between two bookmarks of one signal is drawn as a dimension line in that lane, above the signal, not through it. A circle dragged onto the top quarter of its row (or ↥ in its line) is lifted into a lane above its signal with a leader down to it, so it covers no value ("lift": true in the JSON); dragged lower, it sits on the row again. The laneHeight setting sets its height per label (16 px), and arrowLabels: "overlay" puts the labels back on the arrows. Click a number, in the list or on the waves, to go to that bookmark. Bookmarks and arrows are kept in this browser (localStorage). JSON gives them as "bookmarks" and "arrows" lists for the WaveJSON or a config file, so a generated page and its static SVG carry them; the SVG adds a legend with the texts. Expand / Collapse open or fold every group at once.


Shift+click on the waves puts a second cursor (dashed, on the nearest edge): its Δ to the cursor in time and in cycles of the clock (the selected signal when it is a clock, else the nearest *clk*) is on the axis and in the readout; x swaps the cursors, Esc clears it; keys 1…9 go to that bookmark, and each bookmark's line shows its Δ from the cursor. Shift+drag on the waves zooms into the range dragged over (as the middle button). Ctrl+drag measures a range: its ends snap to the edges of the signal under the pointer (alt: not), it is shaded with its Δ on the axis and in the readout, counted on that signal: a bit's rising edges (its cycles), a bus's changes and the values it took; ctrl+click clears it. The right button does the same and opens a menu: zoom to the range, bookmarks at its ends with the Δ on an arrow, copy Δ, copy image (PNG) and save image as PNG or SVG — the range of the rows on the screen (or of the highlighted rows) as the viewer draws them, with names and axis and the page's colours put in —, save as WaveDrom or copy it (those signals over the range as WaveJSON, a cell per cycle of their clock, buses as = with their values), and for the row under the pointer its colour, radix (a bus of the dump), highlight, hide (a module: all of it; Hidden: N ✕ in the bar shows them again), fold, a bookmark there and its path (on a highlighted row: the colour, radix and hide of every highlighted row; on a selected run — shift+click a name, or alt+drag a box over time and rows, which also opens the menu — of every selected one, and the picture and WaveDrom take that run); fold or open every module (contextMenu: false: the browser's menu). The radix and the hidden rows are kept in this browser and in the saved state.


Bus transactions. With wavefw/bus.js (generated pages have it) the viewer finds the bus ports of every module by their signal names — one port's signals share a prefix (s_axi_awvalid, m_axis_tdata, wb_cyc_o; none: PSEL) — and takes them apart into transfers at the rising edges of the port's clock: AXI4 / AXI4-Lite / AXI3 (writes AW + W burst + B and reads AR + R beats, matched by ID, out of order too), AXI4-Stream packets, APB (setup, wait states, access), AHB / AHB-Lite (pipelined address and data phases, bursts, waits), Avalon-MM (waitrequest, pipelined reads), Avalon-ST packets, Wishbone classic and pipelined, OBI. Each port is a group ⇄ name · protocol · counts under its module: folded, its transfers on the header row (writes blue, reads green, errors red); open, a row of transfers with their labels (W 0x40 ← 0xCAFE ×4) and a row per channel where each beat runs from its valid to the handshake. A click selects a transfer (the cursor at its start, its range measured, its signals highlighted); ]t / [t go to the next / previous one. Ports with names of their own: the setting buses.


Save in the bar's state group downloads what you did in that viewer as one JSON file (<title>.view.json): the time window and the cursor, the order and its prefixes, the folded modules, the highlighted signals, the paint, the selected signal, the search, the bookmarks and arrows, and with sva.js the assertions (the files loaded or edited in the page with their text, the bindings) and the ƒ rows. Load… reads such a file back into a viewer, the same page after a reload or another browser; on other waves it takes what they have and says how many paths it left out. The file's "config" holds settings (sort, sortIgnore, fold, highlight, findHighlight, findOpen), so it pastes into a config file as it is; times are in model units (ps).








Edge styles and radix


The style setting draws edges WaveDrom-like (wavedrom: slanted 1-bit edges, <> bus ends) or like a simulator's wave window (questa: vertical edges, narrow bus crossings; square: vertical edges, square bus ends). auto, the default, slants WaveDrom wave strings and draws exact tv transitions vertically. The radix setting (auto | hex | dec | signed | bin | oct | ascii | float, and fixed point q<F> / sq<F> with F fraction bits: q4, sq15) formats bus values from tv; auto shows up to 6 bits in decimal and wider buses in hex. Both settings also exist per signal ("style", "radix" in the signal object) and override the global value. SystemVerilog assertions


With wavefw/sva.js (and the engine, wavefw/sva.wasm.js, WebAssembly) a viewer gets an assertions group in its bar and, once there are assertions, a panel under the bar: a table with two lines per numbered assertion: its name and the full path of the instance it is bound to (a long one wraps after its dots and copies as one line) with its buttons, under them its kind and property (wrapped at its operators, |->, |=>, ##, intersect, throughout, …; its tooltip: the file, the line and the signals it needs), the results and the binding's buttons. Load file… reads .sv / .sva files into the page (parsed here, kept in this browser). An assertion is checked once it is bound to an instance: drag its line onto a module in the names column (while dragging, the modules that have all its signals turn green, the others fade and say what they miss), or press Bind… for the list of those modules. Names resolve in the instance (exact name, then the shown name of an alias, then without the sortIgnore prefixes; u_fifo.full below it); the clock may also come from above. The engine samples the bound rows at the clock edges, and the line shows the pass / fail counts; Go to first failure moves the view there, Evaluate runs it again, Unbind and ✕ remove it. The same assertion can be bound to several instances. A config file lists the files in "sva" and the bindings in "bind" (mkhtml.py evaluates them over the dump, so the page opens with the results); the panel's JSON button, and the bookmarks' one, write that list. Below: the assertion is bound in the WaveJSON ("sva") to the top of the diagram and evaluated when the page opens; one request is answered too late. The panel adds the numbers: a totals line, Table (one row per binding: counts, first and last failure, longest attempt, latency), Failures (every failed attempt by the time it was found) and the downloads report.md / report.json (sva report reads the second).


A bound assertion with results becomes a group of rows under the module it is bound to (here: under the top's signals), ⚑ a_req_ack · 2 pass · 1 fail, that folds, sorts, is found and highlighted like a module. Its attempts row has a bracket per attempt from its first to its last cycle, ticks at both ends (green pass; a failure green up to its last matched step and red from there to where it failed; grey disabled; an active or capped one open at its end, striped; a bar where attempts crowd; up to three rows, more are merged with a count) under a thin strip with one mark per cycle, so failures show as red ticks even when the whole dump is in view. One row per sequence step follows (1. req, 2. ##[1:3] ack): every attempt's match of that step is a numbered circle at its cycle, a failure a red ✕ where the attempt failed. Arrows join the steps of an attempt (the number of cycles between them on the arrow; a dashed red one to the failure, labelled with the reason), for every attempt on screen when there are fewer than 20, else for the selected one. Click a bar or a circle, Go to first failure, a failure button in go to (up to six) or the keys ]f / [f (next / previous failure); a click on an assertion's line in the panel (again: its next attempt) or ]a / [a (next / previous assertion) goes to its first failure, else its first pass: the view moves to the attempt at the zoom it has, the attempt in the middle, the cursor at the attempt's start, its rows open, its arrows are drawn in front and the bound signals are highlighted; Esc clears it. The selected attempt is also drawn on the signals each step reads (the names in the step's label: 1. req on req), numbered circles joined by arrows, and its start and end are dashed lines across the rows with flags in the group's band: ▶ start and how it ended, ✓ pass, ✕ fail or … not ended (still active at the end of the dump). The bound signals follow the steps (= req; setting svaSignals). The static figure below draws the same rows with every attempt's arrows and a legend of the steps, and the first failure (else the first attempt) of each assertion on its signals with its flags.


With an attempt selected the panel shows Why: every boolean of the assertion taken apart into its operands, one column per cycle of the attempt, a ring where a step matched and the failing cell in red; a cycle's header moves the cursor. The expression field in the bar takes any expression of the signals as in an assertion (req && !ack; @(posedge clk) … samples it at the clock): Enter / shift+Enter go to the next / previous time it becomes true, + Row draws it as a signal in ƒ expressions; the WaveJSON's "sva": {"exprs": […]} brings the two rows of this example.







Assertions on a real protocol: the APB example


The checks of examples/apb/apb.sva bound to the APB testbench (docs/apb_waves.js, made by make demo: the clock-sampled view of the example with the results of sva eval in its "sva"). One of them, apb_demo_no_wait (PSEL && PENABLE |-> PREADY), is deliberately wrong: the slave stretches reads with wait states. Its group is open, the passing ones are folded (fourteen at one place); press ]f on the waves to select its first failure, or open the panel's Table and Failures. The figure under it draws apb_read_waits (SETUP && read |=> !PREADY[*2] ##1 PREADY) as a static SVG; its legend lists the steps: 1 is the antecedent (ante: the read's SETUP phase), 2 and 3 the consequent (cons: two cycles without PREADY, then PREADY). The circles carry these numbers, the arrows the cycles between the steps (1T).

Clock-sampled, plain WaveDrom


With --clock clk the converter samples every rising edge and writes ordinary wave strings, which stock WaveDrom also renders. --gap 100ns:250ns folded 15 cycles into one | cell (--auto-gap 16:2 folds every stretch of 16 cycles or more where nothing but a clock changes, 2 kept on each side). The time.skip entry keeps the time axis correct in the viewer.

For an AI agent: the MCP server


tools/wavemcp.py serves the same reader and the assertion engine to an AI agent over MCP (stdio): claude mcp add wavefw -- python3 -I tools/wavemcp.py (or codex mcp add …). Its tools answer in short JSON: wave_info, wave_signals, wave_values, wave_changes, wave_sample (a row per clock cycle, sampled before the edge), wave_find (an SV expression or sequence per cycle, or a signal's edges and values), wave_stats, sva_check, wave_page (this viewer, or WaveJSON, to show a person). make mcp-bench measures what it saves an agent (docs/USAGE.md § AI agents).

Settings (config file)


Colours, sizes, fonts, line widths and sorting are settings. They are merged in this order, later wins: built-in defaults, the global settings (wavefw/wavefw.config.js loaded before wavefw.js, which sets window.WAVEFW_CONFIG, or WaveFW.configure()), the diagram's "config" object, and the element's data-* attributes or the constructor options; last, the edge style picked in a viewer's bar, kept in this browser for that viewer (picking the settings' own style drops it). A key that is no setting is a console warning (the tools refuse it). The "config" section of a converter config file (examples/demo/demo.config.json, work/dump.config.json) is written into the generated JSON; tools/mkhtml.py uses the top-level one as the page's global settings. This page loads wavefw.config.js.

KeyMeaning


time: {t0, cycle, t1?, skip?}Cycle c starts at t0 + c·cycle ps. skip: [[k, n]] means the | cell at index k stands for n cycles.


signal[i].tv: [[t, v], …]Exact transitions in model time. 1-bit values are 0 1 x z; buses are hex strings. Takes precedence over wave.

signal[i].fieldsA packed struct dumped as one vector: [{"name": "state", "msb": 15, "lsb": 13, "radix"?, "enum"?}, …], the fields MSB first. The signal becomes a foldable {} row with its whole value, a row per field below (its bits, radix and labels). mkconfig.py --sv writes them from a typedef struct packed of the sources.

signal[i].analogtrue or a number of rows (3 for true, at most 12): the bus drawn as a step plot that many rows high, between its smallest and largest value in its radix (signed, float, fixed point), the area under it shaded, x / z as a gap; the menu's Analog / As a bus in a viewer (kept in this browser and in the saved state).


signal[i].enumColorsThe fill of this signal's values by label or number, over the setting enumColors (below in the settings).


signal[i].kind"real" or "string" (a VCD real / string variable, written by the converter and the page's VCD reader): the tv values are texts shown as they are — a real's number, a string —, null unknown; a real can be drawn analog.


signal[i].heightThe row's own height in px (a config's per-signal "height").


signal[i].stroke, fillCSS colours for this signal's trace/outline and bus fill. The name is drawn in the stroke colour.


config.theme, config.colors, config.*Theme, palette (same keys as data-colors) and any other setting for this diagram.


bookmarks: [{n, t, signal, text, color}]Numbered events: t in model units (or a time string), signal the full path (group.name), a CSS colour (default: the accent). Static SVGs add a legend with the texts.


arrows: [{from, to, shape, head, line, color, label}]Arrows between bookmarks n: shape - ~ -~ ~- -| |- -|- (default ~), head > <> < or "" (default >), line solid dashed dotted.

markers / jumps / cursor{t | cycle, label} and {t | cycle, span, label}; t is in model units or a time string. Without jumps, at most six markers become the go to buttons; a jump's span defaults to a fifth of the whole time.
