Bus transactions

A bus is read as transfers, not as forty wiggling signals: write 0x40 ← 0xCAFE, 3 cycles. The viewer finds the bus ports of every module by the names of their signals — AXI4, AXI4-Lite, AXI3, AXI4-Stream, APB, AHB, Avalon-MM, Avalon-ST, Wishbone, OBI — takes them apart at the clock edges and draws each port as a group of transfers and channel beats, where a stall is plain to see. Nothing to configure for the usual names; a setting for your own.

What it looks like

Each port found is a group ⇄ name · protocol · counts (⇄ APB · APB · 1 R · 2 W · 1 err) under its module. It starts folded: its transfers sit on the header row itself, one box each, so a folded port costs one row and still shows when something happens on the bus.

Open it (a click on its name, or Expand) and it has:

  • transactions: the transfers again, stacked in up to three lanes where they overlap, with their labels;
  • one row per channel — AW, W, B, AR, R for AXI; address / data for AHB; … — where every beat is a box from the cycle its valid came to the edge of its handshake. A beat that waits for ready is a long box: the stall is its length.

The colours are the CSS custom properties --bus-write, --bus-read, --bus-error, --bus-channel, one set per theme. The value column shows the transfer — or the beat — at the cursor.

Going through the transfers

Hover a transfer for its fields, its cycles and its times (write 0x8: 0x1234abcd → OKAY · 6 cycles, 35 ns → 95 ns; a beat: its channel, its label and its two times). Click it to select it:

  • the cursor goes to its start (the view moves when it is out of sight);
  • its range from start to end is measured: shaded, its Δ on the axis;
  • the port's signals and its clock are highlighted, everything else dimmed;
  • the title line says what it was: AXI4-Lite: W 0x8 ← 0x1234abcd · 6 cycles, with · error for a failed one.

]t / [t on the waves select the next / previous transfer of any port from the cursor, round the ends, and scroll its row into view. Esc clears the selection with the rest. A saved viewer state (Save) keeps the selected transfer.

How a port is found

For every scope of the dump (every module instance), the viewer looks at its signals' names, lower-cased:

  1. A prefix. The signals of one port share a prefix that ends with _, or have none: s_axi_awvalid, m_axis_tdata, wb_cyc_o, or plain PSEL. Two ports in one module (s_axi_, m_axi_) are two groups.
  2. A direction suffix _i, _o, _in, _out is ignored (awvalid_i is awvalid).
  3. The signals that make the protocol. One of the sets in the table below must be there whole; the rest of the roles are taken when they exist.
  4. The clock: the prefix with the protocol's clock name (s_axi_aclk), else that name in the module, else in the modules above, else a 1-bit *clk of the module. No clock, no port.

Values are sampled just before each rising edge of that clock — what a flop samples, as the assertions and the sva CLI do. A port seen again at another level of the hierarchy (the same nets) is drawn once.

The labels read the same in every protocol: W 0x40 ← 0xCAFE a write of that data to that address, R 0x8 → 0x12 a read and its data, ×4 a burst of four beats.

Ports with other names

When your signals are not named after the protocol, list the port in the buses setting (in the config's "config", a view's "config", or wavelens.config.js):

"config": {
  "buses": [
    { "protocol": "axis", "scope": "tb.dut", "name": "pixels",
      "map": { "tvalid": "pix_valid", "tready": "pix_ready", "tdata": "pix_data", "tlast": "pix_eol" } },
    { "protocol": "apb", "scope": "tb.dut.u_cfg", "prefix": "cfg_", "clock": "pclk_cfg" }
  ]
}
KeyMeaning
protocolaxi axis apb ahb avmm avst wb obi
scopethe instance whose signals they are (the dump's path, e.g. tb.dut)
prefixthe common prefix of standard role names (cfg_ + psel …)
mapinstead of a prefix: role → the signal's name in the scope ("tlast": "pix_eol")
clockthe clock's name in the scope or its full path (default: found as above)
namethe group's name (default: the module and the prefix)

A listed port must still have one of the protocol's sets of signals (the "Found by" column). The listed ports come first, so their names win over the ones found. "busDetect": false turns the search by names off and keeps only the listed ports — useful when a name collides with a protocol's (a valid / data pair that is no bus).

A port is looked for among the signals of one scope of the dump, whatever groups a view puts them in: the AXI-Lite example draws its channels in groups aw, w, b, ar, r and still has one port. In hand-written WaveJSON, signals without a path belong to the whole diagram, one scope.
