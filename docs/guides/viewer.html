Guide

The viewer in the browser

Every interactive waveform of WaveLens — a page built from a dump, a WaveDrom block in your own page, a VCD dropped on wavelens/viewer.html — is the same viewer: a bar of tools, a names column, the waves on a canvas, the bookmark lists under them. This guide goes through it part by part: how to move in time, put cursors, measure, find and highlight signals, mark events, and take pictures out of it. Navigation follows Questa's wave window; the keys of the lists and the text follow vim.

Opening waves

There are three ways to get waves into a viewer:

  • A page built from a dump. make html WAVES=work/my.vcd (or an FST) writes work/my.config.json and work/my.html: one self-contained file with every view of the config, each a viewer. How the page and its config are made is the subject of Dump → page.
  • A dump read in the page. Open wavelens/viewer.html from the repository (file://, nothing to build) and drop a VCD on it, or press Open… in the dump group of its bar. Every viewer has that group, a built page too, so you can load another dump into any of them.
  • WaveDrom JSON in your own page. A <script type="application/wavedrom"> block becomes a viewer; see WaveDrom.

The VCD is read in the browser: its signals grouped by scope (struct, union and interface scopes marked), with their exact transitions. A large dump is cut to the dumpBudget setting (3 000 000 value changes by default): the scope levels nearest the top are kept first, and the toast in the title line says how many signals came in; the find counts the ones left out (no match · 1 not in the page, their names in its tooltip), so a missing signal is not taken for one the simulator did not dump. An FST is binary and is not read in the page: convert it with sva vcd x.fst --out x.vcd (the CLI in bin/, or GTKWave's fst2vcd x.fst > x.vcd), or build a page with make html WAVES=x.fst.

⟳ reads the same file again after a new simulation and keeps the view: the window, the cursor, the folding, the highlight and the bookmarks. In Chrome and Edge the file is picked through the File System Access API, so ⟳ reads what is on the disk now; other browsers ask for the file again. A saved viewer state (.view.json, see Save and Load) dropped on a viewer is loaded instead of read as a dump.

Anatomy of a viewer

  1. The title line: the view's title, short messages (toasts: what a click did, a transfer selected, a dump read), at the right the cursor and the width of the view (cursor 5 ns · view 200 ns; with a range or a second cursor also its Δ) and the ? button.
  2. The bar: one line per kind of work, the groups described below.
  3. The names column: one row per signal, groups (modules, protocol channels, assertion groups, bus ports) as foldable header rows. The ○ mark in front of a name highlights it. Hover a name for its full path.
  4. The value column next to the names: each signal's value at the cursor, in its radix (a long value cut to half the column, its tooltip has all of it).
  5. The waves: the time axis on top, the cursor as a solid orange line, markers dashed, bookmarks as numbered circles and arrows between them. The pointer over a signal shows its value there, the change into it and the next one.
  6. The lists of bookmarks and arrows, when the view has some (Bookmarks and arrows).
  7. The hint line: the mouse and the keys in one paragraph ("hint": false leaves it out).

The keys work once the waves have the focus: click the waves or a name first. With ctrl, alt or ⌘ held the keys stay the browser's (ctrl+S saves the page, it does not sort), except ctrl+C, ctrl+F, ctrl+G, ctrl+Z / ctrl+shift+Z / ctrl+Y (undo, redo; in a text field they are the field's), alt+↑ ↓ and alt+shift+↑ ↓ (the order of your groups). A two-key sequence (gg, zM, yy, ]m …) is two keys within a second.

Only the tools you use

The right button on the bar lists its tool groups with a tick each: a click shows or hides one, Show all and Hide all do them all. The menu stays open while you pick; Esc or a click elsewhere closes it. A line of the bar with no group left goes, so a bar of the cursor and the find is two lines high. A hidden find still floats over the waves on / or ctrl+F. The choice is kept in this browser for this viewer (the setting toolsHidden, a list of group names, starts a page that way).

Tooltips and the keys box

Hover any button, field or list of the bar for what it does and its keys; the tooltip shows after 350 ms (in the right button's menu after 2 s, the menuTipDelay setting, so that it does not get in the way while you read the menu).

? at the right of the title line (key ?) opens the box of every key and mouse action, the plugins' too (bus transactions, assertions). Esc, ? or ✕ close it.

The cursor

A click on the waves puts the cursor there and selects the signal under the pointer. When an edge of that signal is within 8 px of the click (cursorSnap), the cursor lands exactly on the edge; else on a round time of about a pixel at the zoom you have. alt+click never snaps. The cursor reads exactly at any zoom (cursor 1.234567 µs), and the value column shows every signal's value there. shift+← / shift+→ move the cursor a hundredth of the view.

The cursor group places it by number: type a time (1.2us: a unit typed wins over the unit list, or a number in the listed unit) and press Set or Enter. The span field and Show give a window that wide centred on the cursor. A cycle diagram (clock-sampled without time) counts in cycles.

A second cursor

shift+click on the waves (or Second cursor here in the right button's menu) puts a dashed second cursor, snapped to an edge like the first. The axis and the title line then show the distance to the cursor in time, its frequency, and in cycles of a clock: the clock of the cycles band when it is on, else the selected signal's when it is a clock, else the nearest *clk*. x swaps the two cursors; Esc clears the second one.

Clock cycles

A failure is often told as “cycle 147 after reset”, not as a time. Cycles ▸ in the right button's menu shows the cycles of a clock in a band under the time axis: every rising edge a tick, numbered from cycle 0 (as many numbers as fit), the clock named in the header of the names. Show the cycles counts the 1-bit *clk* / *clock* found, Cycles of PCLK on a 1-bit row counts that one, Cycle 0 at this edge makes the rising edge at or before the time clicked cycle 0 (the end of reset, a trigger), Reset the cycles gives back the page's settings. With the band on, the title line says the cursor's cycle (cycle 6 ↑PCLK) and the second cursor's Δ counts that clock. Type c147 (or cycle 147, c-3) in the cursor field to go to that cycle's edge; it works with the band off too. The pictures (PNG, SVG) draw the band; the settings are cycles, cycleClock, cycleZero, and the command is {"op": "cycles", "on": true, "clock": "PCLK", "zero": "50ns"}.

Compare two runs

Compare… in the bar's dump group reads a second run's VCD over these waves: a red band where a signal differs, ≠ on its name, a struck name for a signal the second dump lacks, ]d / [d to the next / previous difference, ≠ N ✕ to stop. On exact waves only. The whole story with pictures: Compare two runs.

Edges and values

Select a signal (click its name or its row; ↑ ↓ or j k select the previous / next one, gg / G the first / last). Then step through its edges, as in Questa:

  • Tab / shift+Tab, ▶ / ◀, or ]e / [e: the cursor to its next / previous edge, the view follows. With no signal selected, ◀ ▶ take the edges of every shown signal (and Tab moves the focus on, as usual).
  • The list between ◀ and ▶ picks the kind (e cycles it): ↕ edge (any change; every value change on a bus), ↑ rise, ↓ fall, = value, ✕ X / Z.
  • = value opens a field: 0x40, 'h40, 8'd64, 64, -3, 'b101, a literal with x / z compared bit for bit ('b10x1, 8'hx5), or a text such as an enum label (ACCESS). Enter / shift+Enter (or ▶ ◀) go to the next / previous time the selected signal becomes that value (none selected: any shown signal).
  • X / Z finds the next / previous x or z of the selected signal (a bus with x or z bits too) or of any shown one: the first X after reset, from the reset's edge.

Measuring a range

ctrl+drag on the waves measures: the range's ends snap to the edges of the signal under the pointer (hold alt: they do not), the range is shaded, and its Δ shows on the axis and in the title line, with what the signal did in it:

  • a bit: its rising edges and the frequency of a period that long (Δ 20 ns / 50 MHz · 2 ↑ PCLK);
  • a bus: its changes and the number of values it took (Δ 80 ns / 12.5 MHz · 7 changes of PADDR (4 values)).

The signal is the one the range was measured on, else the selected one. ctrl+click or Esc clears it.

A picture or a WaveDrom file is what the viewer shows: fold a module, hide rows, highlight the few you want or select a run, measure the range — then save. The static SVG of a page draws the same way.

Selecting and highlighting rows

A run of rows

shift+click a name: every signal from the last one clicked to it is selected (tinted, nothing dimmed, the tint inside bus values too). alt+drag on the waves draws a box: its time becomes the range, the signals it touches are selected, and the menu opens. The menu's colour, radix and Hide then apply to the whole run, and the picture and WaveDrom take it.

Highlight

The ○ mark in front of a name, or ctrl+click on it, puts that signal — or a whole instance, on a module row — in front: the mark fills, the rest is dimmed (dim, 0.3) but stays readable. ctrl+shift+click highlights every row from the last one to it. Several instances at once take different colours. Highlight: N ✕ in the bar or Esc clears it; "highlight": ["tb.dut.u_tx"] in the config starts a page that way.

Find

The find field (/ or ctrl+F) matches the typed letters in order anywhere in a name, as fzf does: awvd finds AWVALID. A word with a . runs over the full path (dev.fifo); words separated by spaces must all match. Matched letters are underlined and the count shows next to the field.

  1. Type the query; Enter, › or n go to the next match, shift+Enter, ‹ or N to the previous one, each scrolled to the middle of the waves.
  2. Highlight (on by default) dims the rows without a match; Open (on by default) folds every module without one.
  3. Paint colours the found signals (and the highlighted ones) in the colour next to it — kept in this browser; Unpaint takes it off. Copy copies the found rows' full paths, one per line.
  4. Mask leaves only the found rows on the waves, with the modules on the way to them (a found module with what is in it). The search still runs over every row; ending it, or Mask off, brings them all back.
  5. Select selects the found signals, as shift+click does (ctrl+click on it: adds them to the selection there was). The right button on one of them then acts on all of them: New group of the N, colour, radix, Hide, the picture.
  6. Esc ends the search; the folding from before it comes back, with the way to the match you stepped to kept open.

Three toggles change how a word matches, as in grep or VS Code:

Hierarchy and folding

In a page made from a whole dump ("hierarchy": true, what mkconfig.py writes) every scope is a group: module rows are bold on a darker band, and neighbouring modules alternate between two backgrounds (moduleShading), so you see where one instance ends. A click on a module row folds or opens it; Expand / Collapse in the list group open or fold all of them.

KeysFold

The settings fold (groups folded at start: true, a depth, or paths), groupOrder (signals first, sub-modules first, or the file's order) and groupColors (one colour per module or channel) shape this further; see Dump → page.

The Modules panel and Only

t (Modules in the list group) opens the page's modules as a tree over the viewer. A click on a module goes to its signals; a box drawn over a few keeps Only those on the waves, the hierarchy around them kept as headers. Only: N modules ‖ in the bar pauses it, ✕ clears it. The whole story with pictures: Modules.

Your groups

The right button's Groups ▸ puts the signal clicked (or the highlighted, the selected ones) Into a group of your own or a New group: a folder at the top of the names with its signals in your order. They stay in their modules too; radix, colour, bookmarks and highlight are the signal's. Out takes them out, the header's Delete this group removes it, Go to (or ctrl+G) opens one, brings it to the middle and selects its first signal.

The order is yours. alt+↑ / alt+↓ move the selected signal (a selected run of them, as one block) one place in its group, or the row of the names with the focus (a group's header: its group); alt+shift+↑ / alt+shift+↓ move the whole group among yours. Drag a signal of a group, or a group's header, in the names: a line shows where it goes — between two signals, at the end of another group of yours (it moves there), or before another group. Each move is one step to undo. A drag over the other names selects their text, as before (ctrl+C copies the paths). The groups are kept in this browser and the saved state; the command is {"op": "group", "name": "ctl", "signals": ["PSEL", "PENABLE"]}, "move": "up" for its place.

Sorting

Sort (s) cycles off → A→Z → Z→A. The prefixes in the field next to it (i_,o_) are skipped when names are compared and drawn dimmed, so i_data sorts under d. Groups move as a block and are sorted inside. The settings: sort, sortIgnore, sortButton.

Packed structs

A packed struct is one row {} with its whole value and a row per field under it. It comes from an FST written by Verilator with --trace-structs (a struct scope of the dump), or from mkconfig.py --sv, which reads typedef struct packed from the sources and writes the "fields" of a vector.

Bookmarks and arrows

A bookmark is a numbered circle on a signal at a time, with a colour and a text: the event you want someone to look at. An arrow joins two bookmarks with a label — a latency, a cause and its effect.

  1. Select a signal, put the cursor near the event and press + Mark or m: a bookmark, snapped to an edge of that signal. Bookmark here in the right button's menu puts one where you clicked.
  2. Type its text in the list under the waves; change its time (250ns, 85.12us), its signal (empty: the time axis) or its colour there. cursor moves it to the cursor; the Δ column shows its distance from the cursor.
  3. Drag a circle on the waves to move it. shift+drag from one circle to another draws an arrow; or + Arrow joins the last two.
  4. Pick the arrow's shape (- ~ -~ ~- -| |- -|-: straight, curve, curve late / early, corner, step), head (→ end, ↔ both, ← start, none), line (solid, dashed, dotted), colour and label.

Arrow labels get a thin lane of their own between the signals, so they cover no value; an arrow between two bookmarks of one signal is a dimension line in that lane. A circle dragged onto the top quarter of its row (or ↥ in its line) is lifted into the lane above the signal with a leader down to it ("lift": true in the JSON).

1 … 9 go to that bookmark (its signal selected), ]m / [m to the next / previous one in time; a click on a number in the list does the same. The bookmarks are kept in this browser; JSON gives the "bookmarks" and "arrows" lists to paste into a view of the config, so the built page — and its static SVG — carries them. Clear forgets this browser's bookmarks and arrows (the page's own come back on reload; your markers stay).

Save and Load

Save downloads what you did in the viewer as <title>.view.json: the time window and the cursor, the order and its prefixes, the folded modules, the highlight, the paint, the selected signal, the search, the bookmarks and arrows, the radix, analog and hidden rows, the second cursor and the range, the gap settings, the assertions (files loaded or edited in the page, with their text, and the bindings) and the ƒ rows. Load… (or dropping the file on a viewer) reads it back — after a reload, in another browser, or on other waves: what they lack is left out and counted in the title line. A state says what is not there too ("cursor2": null, "range": null, the edge kind): loaded, it clears them; a key a state leaves out (an older one, one written by hand) keeps what the viewer has.

The file's "config" (sort, sortIgnore, fold, highlight, the find toggles …) pastes into a config file as it is; times are in model units (ps).

Save page downloads the page itself, <page>.view.html, with your view written into it: one file to send. Whoever opens it — in another browser, on another machine — sees the bookmarks, arrows, groups, colours, highlight, cursor and window, the assertions and ƒ rows you had; the waves are the page's as they were, the live link to an agent and its token are left out. It is the page's edits block that tools/viewedit.py writes, a new revision: what the person then changes stays theirs. It is there on a page that is all in itself (make html, mkhtml.py); a page with wavelens.js beside it, or a dump opened in the page (Open…), keeps its view with Save. The file carries what you did: your assertion files' text, notes, the search.

Undo and redo

ctrl+Z (or ↶ in the bar's title line, there with the tools folded too) takes back the last change of what you edit in a viewer: bookmarks and arrows, markers, your groups, colours, radix, analog and hidden rows, the highlight, the edge style, the scroll and the folded tools, the gaps and the cycles. ctrl+shift+Z or ctrl+Y (↷) does it again. A step is what one action changed: a menu item, a colour picked, a bookmark dragged, a batch of commands from a script or a live agent; the toast names it (undone: radix, hidden). The cursor, the window, the selection and the search are not steps (move them back with l, the bookmarks, Esc). A text typed (a bookmark's, an arrow's label) is one step when its field is left; in a text field ctrl+Z is the field's own. On another keyboard layout the keys go by their place (ctrl+Я is ctrl+Z). The history starts when the page opens (with the page's edits in) and keeps 100 steps; a new change after an undo drops what could be redone.

Themes

Three themes: light (warm paper), white (black traces, for print and slides) and dark. They follow the system (prefers-color-scheme) unless set: the theme button in a generated page's header, <html data-theme="white">, WaveLens.setTheme('dark'), or "config": {"theme": …}. Every colour is a CSS custom property; see Dump → page for colours per signal, group and module.

Copying, Esc and printing

  • yy or ctrl+C on the waves copies the selected signal's full path (or the highlighted ones'); during a search ctrl+C ends the search instead. Names selected with the mouse copy as full paths, one per line.
  • Esc anywhere in the viewer clears the search, the highlight and the selection (and a range, a second cursor, a menu); the time window stays.
  • The browser's print (ctrl+P) draws every row of every viewer — not only the part in view — with its title and cursor readout; the bar's tools are left out.
